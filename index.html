Your clients.

How a conversation works

From first call to close, six to twelve months is typical.

  1. 1

    A confidential call

    A first conversation about your practice and what you want next. Nothing to prepare.

  2. 2

    An NDA

    A nondisclosure agreement is signed before any details change hands. Client confidences are shared only as the professional rules allow.

  3. 3

    Fit

    A look at the practice together, and a plain answer on whether it's a match and which path makes sense.

  4. 4

    Terms in writing, including how the price is paid: usually bank financing plus seller participation, such as part of the price paid over time.

  5. 5

    Close

    Documents are signed, and the transition begins on the schedule you agreed.

Questions owners ask

Who will own my firm?

Your firm stays attorney-owned. If you keep practicing, you remain the majority owner, and the minority stake is held by an attorney licensed in your state, never by a company or an outside investor. If you retire, the practice is sold to an attorney licensed in your state under the rules that govern the sale of a law practice.

Do I have to stop practicing?

Not if you choose to partner. You keep running the practice and your client relationships. If you choose to retire, the rules on selling a law practice generally require you to stop practicing privately in the area. The handoff is planned around your timeline.

Is this confidential?

Yes. An NDA is in place before you share any details, and nothing about the conversation is made public. Your staff, clients and referral sources hear about it only when you decide they should. If you sell, the rules then require written notice to clients, and New Jersey also requires a published notice of the sale.

Do you charge sellers anything?

No. There's no fee to talk, to share information under an NDA, or to close. If you partner, what the services team costs is set out in writing before you commit. You'll want your own lawyer and accountant, and their fees are yours.

What if I'm not ready yet?

That's fine. Many owners start talking a year or more before they decide anything. A confidential call costs nothing and commits you to nothing.
