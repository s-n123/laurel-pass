Questions owners ask

Who will own my firm?

Your firm stays attorney-owned. If you keep practicing, you remain the majority owner, and the minority stake is held by a licensed attorney, never by a company or an outside investor. If you retire, the practice is sold to a licensed attorney under the rules that govern the sale of a law practice.

Do I have to stop practicing?

Not if you choose to partner. You keep running the practice and your client relationships. If you choose to retire, the rules on selling a law practice generally require you to stop practicing privately in the area, and the handoff is planned around your timeline.

Is this confidential?

Yes. An NDA is signed before any details change hands, and nothing about the conversation is made public. Your staff, clients and referral sources hear about it only when you decide they should.

Do you charge sellers anything?

No. There's no fee to talk, to share information under an NDA, or to go ahead. You'll want your own lawyer and accountant, and their fees are yours.

What if I'm not ready yet?

That's fine. Many owners start talking a year or more before they decide anything. A confidential call costs nothing and commits you to nothing.
