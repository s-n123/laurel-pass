From first call to closing

In about 90 to 120 days.

  1. 1

    A confidential call

    Thirty minutes about your practice and what you want next. We'll sign an NDA first if you prefer.

  2. 2

    A look at the numbers

    Three years of fee revenue, matter volume and where your clients come from. Nothing you'd need an accountant to prepare.

  3. 3

    A written offer

    A plain-English letter of intent: price, how much is paid at closing, and any transition terms. No exclusivity until you're ready.

  4. 4

    Transition and close

    Client notices go out, and your staff, phone numbers and referral relationships carry over. Then you step back.

Questions owners ask

Who is behind this?

A New York attorney with a background in large-firm M&A and tax. We buy practices to own and run them for the long term, and every practice we acquire stays 100% owned by lawyers.

Will my clients be told?

Yes, in the way the sale-of-practice rules require: a joint written notice, the right to choose other counsel, and their existing fee agreements honored. We prepare the notices with you.

Can I stay on for a while?

Possibly, in a limited role focused on handing off active matters and making introductions. Rule 1.17 ties a sale to retiring from private practice in the area, so we define any role with you and your counsel before signing.

What happens to my staff?

We want them. The people who know your clients and your courts are a large part of what we're buying.

Is this confidential?

Yes. Nobody at your firm, in your courts or among your referral sources hears about a conversation with us unless you decide they should.
