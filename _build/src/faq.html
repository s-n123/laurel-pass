About Laurel Way Partners

What is Laurel Way Partners?

Laurel Way Partners acquires established DWI and criminal-defense law practices in the New York City area from owners ready to step back. We also look at other flat-fee practices, and we buy practices to own and run them for the long term.

Who runs Laurel Way Partners?

Laurel Way Partners is led by a New York attorney with a background in large-firm M&A and tax. You'll know exactly who we are from the first call, and the written notice your clients receive under Rule 1.17 identifies the buyer, including bar admissions and years in practice in New York.

Is Laurel Way Partners related to other firms with "Laurel" in the name?

No. Laurel Way Partners is not affiliated with any other firm using the Laurel name, including investment firms, funds and software companies with similar names.

Are you a broker or a fund?

Neither. We buy practices to own and run them for the long term. The buyer in each sale is a lawyer or a law firm owned entirely by lawyers, as Rules 1.17 and 5.4 require, and every practice we acquire stays 100% owned by lawyers.

What we buy

What kinds of practices do you buy?

DWI and DUI defense first. We also look at misdemeanor criminal defense, traffic and license matters, wills and trusts, uncontested divorce, residential real estate closings and Chapter 7 consumer bankruptcy, as long as most matters are billed at a flat fee or advance retainer.

How big does a practice need to be?

We focus on practices with $500K to $5M in annual fees. If yours is outside that range, get in touch anyway; we'll tell you quickly whether it fits.

Where do you buy practices?

New York City, Long Island and Westchester.

Price, payment and timing

How do you decide what to pay?

We look at three years of fee revenue and profit, where new matters come from, how much of the work depends on you personally, and whether staff and referral sources will stay. Our guide What is my practice worth? explains each factor.

How is the price paid?

Most of the price is paid at closing, with any balance on clear written terms. Our letter of intent states the price, how much is paid at closing, and the terms for anything paid later. If the purchase uses SBA-backed lending, the lender's rules can delay payments on a seller note; the letter of intent spells out when each part is paid.

How is the purchase financed?

Before we sign a letter of intent, we line up a lender, including SBA-backed lending. The lender's final approval comes during diligence and is usually the longest step before closing.

How long does a sale take?

We aim to close in about 90 to 120 days from the first call. Lender review is usually the longest step. See how a sale works for our target timeline.

What do you need to see first?

Three years of fee revenue, matter volume by type and where new clients come from. Summary figures are enough at the start, and none of them identify clients.

Will you sign an NDA?

Yes. We'll sign a nondisclosure agreement before you share anything, if you prefer.

Is this confidential?

Yes. Nobody at your firm, in your courts or among your referral sources hears about a conversation with us unless you decide they should.

Your clients

Will my clients be told?

Yes. New York Rule 1.17 requires seller and buyer to send each client a joint written notice. It explains the client's right to choose other counsel or take the file, that the buyer will honor existing fee agreements, and who the buyer is. We prepare the notices with you. More in What happens to your clients when you sell.

Can my clients go to another lawyer instead?

Yes. Every client can hire other counsel or take their file. A client who takes no action and doesn't object within 90 days after the notice is sent is presumed to consent to the transfer, subject to any court rule or statute that requires more.

Will my clients' fees go up?

Not because of the sale. We honor your existing fee agreements, and the rule bars raising a client's fee by reason of the sale unless the client's retainer agreement permits it or the client agrees.

What happens to open matters and prepaid flat fees?

The purchase agreement says how fees for unfinished work are split between you and the buyer, since the buyer does the remaining work after closing. If a client chooses other counsel, any unearned part of the fee is refunded, and the agreement says in advance who funds that refund.

You and your staff

What happens to my staff?

We want them. The people who know your clients and your courts are a large part of what we're buying.

Can I stay on for a while?

Possibly, in a limited role: a defined transition period focused on handing off active matters and introductions to clients, courts and referral sources. Rule 1.17 ties a sale to retiring from private practice in the area, and we are not aware of a New York ethics opinion on how long, or in what role, a seller may stay. Under the ABA's opinion on the Model Rule, help is limited to transitioning active matters for a reasonable period, with no new matters. We define any role with you and your counsel before signing.

Do I have to stop practicing law?

Not entirely, but you do stop practicing privately in the area. Rule 1.17 treats a sale as part of retiring from private practice where the practice was conducted: the county and city, and any county or city next to it. For a New York City practice that likely means all five boroughs plus Nassau and Westchester, and for a Nassau or Westchester practice it can include New York City. In one NYSBA ethics opinion, a seller who moved to practice in distant upstate counties was treated as retired. Seller and buyer can also agree on reasonable restrictions. Your own counsel should advise on what you plan to do next.

Can my firm name stay on the door?

Often, with care. A 2019 NYSBA opinion let a lawyer already affiliated with a firm buy it under Rule 1.17 and keep its name if that isn't misleading, and a 2020 opinion let a buyer list the former firm's name and dates on its letterhead. Plan the name with your counsel. See Can your firm name stay on the door?

What happens to my phone numbers, website and reviews?

They carry over at closing, along with the firm's Google Business Profile and directory listings, so the practice keeps answering the calls it gets today.

Taxes and advice

How is the sale taxed?

It depends mostly on how the price is allocated, and also on how your practice is organized and where it operates. If you own the practice directly or through an S corporation or partnership, goodwill is generally long-term capital gain, while payments for a non-compete, your work after closing and receivables are generally ordinary income; a practice organized as a C corporation can be taxed twice, and a practice in New York City can owe city business tax on the sale. Should I have my own lawyer and accountant?

Yes. Have your own lawyer review the purchase agreement and your own accountant review the tax side before you sign. Nothing on this site is legal or tax advice.
