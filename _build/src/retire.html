Retire on your timeline

Step back on your timeline, with your clients in good hands.

For owners of elder law, estate planning and probate firms in New York and northern New Jersey who are ready to retire. A fair purchase under the rules that govern the sale of a law practice, your staff kept on, and a structured handoff.

In short: your practice is purchased by a licensed attorney in a sale structured under the professional rules for selling a law practice. Your staff stay on. The handoff to clients and referral sources is planned with you, and the families you've planned for are cared for by lawyers who will still be there when their plans are used.

Who this is for

Owners of elder law, estate planning, and probate and trust administration firms on Long Island, in Westchester, in the five boroughs and in northern New Jersey, with roughly $750K to $3M in annual revenue and one to five attorneys. If you're looking for someone to take over your estate planning practice in New York, or a buyer for a probate practice in New Jersey, this is the path to read about.

A fair purchase, done by the rules

New York and New Jersey each have a professional rule on the sale of a law practice (Rule 1.17 in both states). It governs how a practice is sold, what clients must be told, and their right to choose another lawyer. The purchase is structured to follow it.

The price reflects your practice and is set after a review under an NDA. It's financed with a mix of bank financing and seller participation, and every term is in writing before you commit. There's no charge to you; your own lawyer and accountant should review the terms.

Your clients and their original documents

Estate planning clients trust you with documents that may not be used for decades. Original wills and other documents are handed over with a written record. Each client is told about the sale in writing, as the rules require, and keeps the right to choose another lawyer and take their file.

Your staff

The people who know your clients and your files are a large part of what makes the practice work. The plan is to keep them.

A structured handoff

The handoff is planned around your timeline: introductions to clients, to their families where appropriate, and to the advisors, CPAs and care managers who send you work. The sale-of-practice rules generally require you to stop practicing privately in the area, so any role you keep during the transition is agreed in writing, with your own counsel, before closing.

What stays the same

  • Your name on the door, where the professional rules allow.
  • Your clients, cared for by lawyers who will still be there when their plans are used.
  • Your staff, kept on.

How a conversation works

  1. A confidential call. A first conversation about your practice and when you'd like to step back. Nothing to prepare.
  2. An NDA. A nondisclosure agreement is signed before any details change hands.
  3. Fit. A look at the practice together, and a plain answer on whether it fits.
  4. Structure. The price, the financing, the handoff and the client notice, all in writing.
  5. Close. Documents are signed, clients are notified as the rules require, and the handoff begins.

Six to twelve months is typical from the first call to closing, and the schedule can be set further out if you're planning ahead. If you plan to retire from a practice in Westchester, on Long Island or in northern New Jersey a year or two from now, now is a good time to talk.
